Phase A.0 result correction

Correction to the Phase A.0 interpretation

The original experiment was reported as evidence that the Refiner outperformed naive prompting. A later audit found unequal baseline scoring contexts, seven inputs that differ from their manifest hashes, and ten Arm B specimens with no completed proposal after API failures. Those problems prevent attributing the published comparison to the editing mechanism. We no longer present this run as evidence of comparative effectiveness.

The original results and signature remain available as historical records; the signature establishes artifact integrity, not scientific validity. A new registered experiment is required before renewing the effectiveness claim.

Read the correction decision and reproducible audit.

What the audit found

The experiment attempted to compare two ways of editing a skill's configuration header. Arm A took a model's rewrite; Arm B scored proposed edits and kept those meeting its acceptance rule. The original interpretation assumed that an unchanged instruction body made the rest of the score cancel between the arms.

That assumption missed the judge's use of directory names and supporting files. Arm A used cached baseline scores from the corpus manifest. Arm B rescored the loaded files in isolated temporary directories. All 60 baseline pairs differ, by 7.5667 points on average. A score difference therefore cannot be attributed entirely to the header edit.

Seven recovered inputs also differ from their declared manifest hashes. Ten Arm B specimens have no completed proposal and an initial API failure; they were included as unchanged results even though no edit was evaluated. The historical summary reported zero errors. These observations require an explicit treatment of input identity and failed attempts before a new comparison.

The pinned judge reproduces all 60 retained Arm B baseline scores from recovered inputs. That replay diagnoses the scoring discrepancy; it is not a new model run or a replacement experiment verdict.

The original analysis recorded a 6.68-point gap, a Wilcoxon p-value below 0.000001 and Cohen's d of 1.49. Those calculations use the affected observations; they do not resolve the validity problems above. The former description of the p-value as the probability that the result was a fluke was also incorrect.

The historical verdict was PROCEED. This correction preserves that decision as history. It neither replaces it with DESCOPE nor establishes the opposite effectiveness claim. Current engineering decisions need their own basis.

What remains to be tested

A new registered experiment needs frozen input directories, verified input and judge digests, equivalent baseline scoring contexts, and an explicit policy for retries and incomplete pairs. Its statistical methods must resolve the discrepancies between the old template and implementation. The promised dimension-level Goodhart audit also remains incomplete; acceptance counts cannot substitute for it.
